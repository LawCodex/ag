## PART I

PRELIMINARY
1\. Short title
This Act may be cited as the Weapons of Mass Destruction (Prohibition and Non-Proliferation)
Act 2024.
2\. Interpretation
In this Act
“acquire” means in relation to any items, means the purchase, hire or borrowing of the items,
or the acceptance of the items, as a gift;
“asset” means funds, property, financial resources and economic resources of every kind,

whether tangible or intangible, corporeal or incorporeal, moveable or immovable, actual or
potential, however acquired, including—
(a)

cash; and

(b)

virtual or digital currencies, including crypto currencies; and

(c)

bank credits, travellers’ cheques, bank cheques, money orders; and

(d)

precious metals and precious stones; and

(e)

real property, chattels and vessels; and

(f)

shares, securities, bonds and drafts; and

(g)

rights of set-off, guarantees, performance bonds, and other financial
commitments; and

(h)

letters of credit, bills of lading and bills of sale; and

(i)

instruments of export financing; and

(j)

any other financial resources; and

(k)

natural resources; and

(l)

human resources such as crew services; and

(m)

any other economic resources that may be used to obtain funds, goods
or services; and

(n)

legal documents or instruments in any form, including electronic or digital,
evidencing title to, or interest in, or right to claim an asset; and

(o)

any interest, dividend, income or value accruing from, generated by, or derived
from an asset;

“authorized officer” means designated enforcement officers under a relevant Authority,
which may set conditions for the conduct of activities by authorized officers;
“biological weapon” means the following, together or separately
(a) microbial or other biological agents, or toxins whatever their origin or method of
production, of types and in quantities that have no justification for prophylactic,
protective or other peaceful purposes;

(b) weapons, equipment or means of delivery designed to use such agents or toxins for
hostile purposes or in armed conflict;
“Biological Weapons Convention (1972)” means the Convention on the Prohibition of the
Development, Production and Stockpiling of Bacteriological (Biological) and Toxin Weapons
and on their Destruction;

“brokering” means the activity of a person who, acting as an agent on behalf of another
person—
(a)

negotiates, arranges for or facilitates the purchasing, financing, insurance, storage,
warehousing, freight-forwarding, sale or supply of items; or

(b)

buys, sells or supplies such items;

“chemical weapon” means the following, together or separately—
(a)

Toxic chemicals and their precursors, except where intended for purposes not
prohibited under the Chemical Weapons Convention, as long as the types and
quantities are consistent with such purposes;

(b) Munitions and devices, specifically designed to cause death or other harm through
the toxic properties of those toxic chemicals specified in subparagraph (a), which
would be released as a result of the employment of such munitions and devices;
(c) Any equipment specifically designed for use directly in connection with the
employment of munitions and devices specified in paragraph (b).
“Chemical Weapons Convention (1993)” means the Convention on the Prohibition of the
Development, Production, Stockpiling and use of Chemical Weapons and on their Destruction
(including the annexes to that Convention).
“controlled items” means any items prescribed as controlled items under section 7;
“develop” in relation to any nuclear, biological and chemical weapon, means any stage prior
to the serial production of the weapons, including design, design research, design analysis,
development of a design concept, assembly and testing of a prototype, pilot production,
generation of design data, the process of transforming design data into a product,
configuration design, integration design, and layout;
“entity” means any government agency, academic institution, corporation, company,
partnership, society, association, firm, sole proprietorship, or other legal entity;

“export” means to take out from Antigua and Barbuda items by land, water or air, and
includes the placing of the goods in a conveyance for the purpose of taking the items out from
Antigua and Barbuda; but does not include the taking out from Antigua and Barbuda of items
that have been brought in transit or transhipped;
“finance” refers to the act of providing funds or financial services which are used, in whole
or in part, for the manufacture, acquisition, possession, development, export, trans-shipment,
brokering, transport, transfer, stockpiling or use of nuclear, chemical or biological weapons
and their means of delivery and related materials including both technologies and dual use
goods used for purposes contrary to this Act, or in contravention of any other national laws or,
where applicable, international obligations;
“items” includes any commodities, technology, data, technical assistance, services, software,
processes, activities, facilities, substances, materials, goods, equipment, components,
assemblies or systems;
“manufacture” in relation to any nuclear biological or chemical weapons, means any stage
of production of the goods, including construction, production, engineering, integration,
assembly, mounting, inspection, testing, and quality assurance;
“Minister” means the Minister with responsibility for Foreign Affairs;
“Non-Proliferation Treaty (1968)” means the Treaty on the Non-Proliferation of Nuclear
Weapons;
“nuclear weapon” means an explosive device that derives its destructive force from nuclear
reactions, either fission or from a combination of fission and fusion reactions;
“permit” means any permit issued under this Act and includes a special permit;
“person” means any natural person, legal person, or juridical person including, but not
limited to, entities with legal rights, duties, and obligations;
“proceedings” includes the following:
(a)

Proceedings during an investigative phase;

(b)

Court proceedings aimed at establishing the guilt or innocence of an alleged
perpetrator;

(c)

Post-trial proceedings, extradition proceedings triggered at the request of a foreign
State; and

(d) Bilateral proceedings established for the purpose of rendering mutual assistance in
criminal matters, such as for the taking of statements and those designed to execute
the freezing and forfeiture of assets;
“proliferation” means the illegal manufacture, acquisition development, export, transshipment, brokering, transport, transfer, stockpiling or use of nuclear, chemical, or biological
weapons and their means of delivery and related materials;
“proliferation financing” means the provision of funds or financial services used for the
manufacture, acquisition, possession, development, export, trans-shipment, brokering,
transport, transfer, stockpiling or use of nuclear, chemical or biological weapons and their
means of delivery and related materials (including both technologies and dual-use goods used
for non-legitimate purposes), in contravention of national laws or, where applicable,
international obligations;
“provision of services” related to international trade in controlled items including (i)
Brokering, (ii) Financing, (iii) Technical assistance, (iv) Transmitting technical data, (v)
Transportation;
“re-export” means to take out from Antigua and Barbuda by land, water or air, any items that
have been imported for use within Antigua and Barbuda;
“relevant Authority” means the body of persons appointed in section 18;
“restricted activities” means any activity that is contrary to the purpose of this Act such as
any activity that supports the development, production, handling, usage, maintenance, storage,
inventory or proliferation of any weapon of mass destruction and its delivery system;
“technical assistance” includes instructions, skills, training, the provision of working
knowledge and consulting services and may involve the transfer of technical data;
“technical data” includes blueprints, plans, diagrams, models, formulae, tables, engineering
designs and specifications, manuals and instructions in print or electronic format;
“transit” means to bring items from any country into Antigua and Barbuda territory but not
entering its economy by land, sea, or air, where the items are to be taken out from Antigua
and Barbuda on the same conveyance on which they are brought into Antigua and Barbuda
without any landing in Antigua and Barbuda, but does not include the passage through
Antigua and Barbuda in accordance with international law of a foreign conveyance carrying
items;
“transmit” in relation to any technical data or technology, means to —
(a)

provide in Antigua and Barbuda by electronic means; or

(b)

make available in Antigua and Barbuda on a computer, so that it becomes
accessible (whether on a request, or subject to a pre-condition, or otherwise) to a
person in a foreign country, whether he is a specific person, a person within a
specific class, any person in general or the person who carries out the
transmission;

“transhipment” means to remove items from the conveyance on which they were brought
into the territory of Antigua and Barbuda without entering its economy and to place the items
on the same or another conveyance for the purpose of taking them out of Antigua and
Barbuda’s territory, where these acts are carried out on a through bill of lading, through
airway bill or through manifest;
“unlisted items” means items that may be used in restricted activities but are not prescribed
as controlled items under Section 7;
“United Nations Security Council Resolution 1540 (2004)” means the resolution that was
adopted by the UN Security Council on 28 April 2004;
“use” in relation to any items, means the operation, installation, maintenance, inspection,
repair, overhaul, and refurbishing of the items;
“weapons of mass destruction” means any weapon designed to kill, harm or infect people,
animals or plants through the effect of nuclear explosion or dispersion or the toxic properties
of a chemical weapon or the infectious or toxic properties of a biological weapon, and
includes a delivery system designed, adapted or intended for the deployment of such weapons
and their related materials.
3\. Jurisdiction and application
This Act shall extend—
(a)

to acts or omissions prohibited under this Act, which are committed by any person
or entity in the territory of Antigua and Barbuda;

(b)

to acts or omissions prohibited under this Act, which are committed by any
Antiguan citizen or entity outside the territory of Antigua and Barbuda;

(c)

to acts or omissions prohibited by this Act, which are committed on board Antigua
and Barbuda sea vessels and aircraft;

(d)

to acts or omissions prohibited by this Act, which are committed by an ordinarily
resident in Antigua and Barbuda;

(e)

to acts or omissions prohibited by this Act, which are committed with the intent to
harm Antigua and Barbuda or its nationals or to compel Antigua and Barbuda to
do or abstain from doing any act; or

(f)

to acts or omissions prohibited by this Act, in which the victim of the offence is a
citizen of Antigua and Barbuda.

## PART II

WEAPONS OF MASS DESTRUCTION

4\. Offences related to weapons of mass destruction
(1) A person who knowingly develops, produces, manufactures, otherwise acquires, possesses,
stockpiles, transports, transfers, engages in proliferation or proliferation financing or retains a
nuclear, biological or chemical weapon, commits an offence and shall be punished upon
conviction by twenty-five years imprisonment and a fine not exceeding five million ($5,000,000)
dollars.
(2) A person who knowingly uses a nuclear, biological, or chemical weapon, commits an
offence and shall be punished upon conviction to life imprisonment
(3) A person who knowingly engages in preparations to use any nuclear, biological, or chemical
weapon, commits an offence and shall be punished upon conviction for a term of imprisonment
not exceeding twenty-five years and a fine not exceeding five million ($5,000,000) dollars.
(4) A person who knowingly constructs, acquires, or retains any facility intended for the
production of nuclear, biological or chemical weapon commits an offence and shall be punished
upon conviction for a term of imprisonment not exceeding twenty-five years and a fine not
exceeding five million ($5,000,000) dollars.
5.

Offences related to controlled items

(a)

develops, acquires, manufactures, possesses, stores, transports, or uses controlled
items, without a permit granted under sections 8 and 12;

restricted end-user specified in section 10(1) without a prescribed special permit
granted under section 9(2) and 12;

(d)
exports, transits, trans-ships, or re-exports controlled items or unlisted items to a
prohibited end-user specified in section 10(2);
(e)

carries out an act of brokering of any controlled items without being registered

Act,

commits an offence and shall be liable upon conviction of ten years imprisonment or fined an
amount not exceeding one million ($1,000,000.00) dollars.
6\. Criminal liability
(1) Where a criminal offence under this Act has been committed by an entity, any person who at
the time of the commission of the offence was acting as a director, manager, secretary or other
similar officer of the entity or was purporting to act in any such capacity, or was in any manner or
to any extent responsible for the management of any of the affairs of such entity, or was assisting
in such management, shall be deemed to be guilty of that offence unless the activities giving rise
to the offence were committed without the person’s knowledge or reason to know that a
violation of this Act was occurring, and that the person exercised appropriate due diligence to
prevent the
commission of such offence, having regard to the nature of his functions in that capacity and to all
the circumstances.
(2) A person who aids, abets, counsels, or procures any of the offences prohibited under this Act
commits an offence and shall be liable upon conviction to imprisonment for a period of twenty
years or to a fine not exceeding three million ($3,0000,000) dollars.
(3) A person who conspires to commit any of the offences prohibited under this Act commits an
offence and shall be liable upon conviction to imprisonment for a period of twenty years or to a
fine not exceeding three million ($3,000,000) dollars.
(4) A person who attempts to commit any of the offences prohibited under this Act commits an
offence and shall be liable upon conviction to imprisonment for a period of twenty years or to a
fine not exceeding three million ($3,.000,000) dollars.
7\. Controlled Items
(1) The Minister, after consultations with the relevant authorities, may by order published in the
Gazette, prescribe any items as controlled items for the purposes of this Act.
(2) If any question arises as to whether any item is or is not included in a class of items
appearing in an order made under subsection (1), such question shall be decided by the Minister,
after consultations with the relevant authorities.

8\. Relevant Domestic Activities Related to Controlled Items
(1) Every person that develops, acquires, manufactures, possesses, stores, transports, or uses
controlled items shall be in possession of a prescribed permit from the relevant Authority pursuant
to regulations issued under this Act.
(2) Exemptions from the permit requirement under subsection (1) may be granted under the
regulations in this Act in strict accordance with international obligations.
9\. Relevant International Trade Activities Related to Controlled Items and Unlisted Items
(1) Every person that imports, exports, transits, transships, or re-exports controlled items shall
be in possession of a prescribed permit from the relevant Authority pursuant to regulations issued
under this Act.
(2) Every person that exports, transits, transships, or re-exports controlled items or unlisted
items to a restricted end-user specified in section 10(1) shall be in possession of a prescribed
special permit.
(3) Exemptions from the permit requirement under subsection (1) and (2) may be granted in the
regulations under this Act in strict accordance with international obligations.
(4) Where a person is informed by the relevant Authority or otherwise knows or has reason to
believe that any unlisted items will be used for any restricted activities, then the person shall notify
the relevant Authority of his intention to export, transit, transship or re-export such unlisted item at
least 21 days before that transaction is to be carried out.
(5) Upon receiving such notification, pursuant to subsection (4), the relevant Authority shall
decide whether or not and under what condition to allow that transaction to proceed.
10\. Restricted and Prohibited End Users
(1) The relevant Authority may designate an end-user to be a restricted end-user for which a
special permit is required under this Act.
(2) The relevant Authority may designate an end-user to be a prohibited end user to which all
export, transit, trans-shipment and re-export of controlled items or unlisted items under this Act
are prohibited.
(3) A list of the restricted end-users and prohibited end-users shall be published by order in the
Gazette, and the relevant Authority may amend the list from time to time.

12\. Permits
(1)
Without prejudice to the general application of the related laws, for the purpose of
controlled items regulated under this Act, the relevant Authority shall issue the permits required
for such controlled items and register brokers of such controlled items in accordance with the
provisions of this Act.
(2)
An application for a permit or special permit referred to in sections 8 and 9 shall be made
to the relevant Authority in accordance with the regulations made under this Act.
(3)
The relevant Authority may, at any time after the receipt of an application under
subsection (2) and before the application is determined, by a written notice, require the applicant
to provide any additional documents or information within the period specified in the notice.

(4) If the additional documents or information required under subsection (3) are not provided by
the applicant within the period specified in the notice or any extended period granted by the
relevant Authority, the application shall be deemed to be withdrawn and shall not be further
proceeded with, but without affecting the right of the applicant to make a new application.
(5) The relevant Authority may, after considering the application for a permit under subsection
(2) and any additional documents or information provided under subsection (3) and being satisfied
that all the requirements have been fulfilled, grant the permit in accordance with the regulations
made under this Act.
(6) The relevant Authority shall, after considering the application for a permit under subsection
(2) and any additional documents or information provided under subsection (3), and beingsatisfied

that all the requirements have not been fulfilled, refuse the permit in accordance with the
regulations made under this Act.
(7) The relevant Authority may impose such conditions as it considers appropriate in granting
the permit, including, but not limited to the following—
(a)

the quantities of controlled items provided for by the permit;

(b)

the end-use requirements and the verification of such end-use requirements of
controlled items;
the security and physical protection measures of controlled items;

(c)
(d)

the individuals in a permitted entity who may have access to the controlled items
in case the permit is granted to an entity;

(e) the states or territories to which controlled items may be exported or from which
controlled items may be imported;
(f) the re-export from a state to which controlled items originating in Antigua and
Barbuda are destined for export;
(g)

the ports, airports or border posts which may be used for the import, export, re
export or transit of controlled goods.

(8) Every permit granted under subsection (1) shall state the period for which the permit is valid.
(9) The conditions imposed under the permit may at any time be varied or amended by the
relevant Authority provided that it is necessary for national interest, national security or nonproliferation purposes and the holder of the permit is given a reasonable opportunity of being
heard.
(10) The decision of the relevant Authority under subsection (5) and (6) shall be communicated
to the applicant in writing as soon as practicable.

(11) The written notice by the relevant Authority under subsection (10) shall specify—
(a)

in the case where the permit is granted, the fact of such grant and the conditions, if
any, imposed under subsection (7); and

(b)

(12) A person aggrieved due to a denial of a permit under this section may appeal to the relevant
Authority, who may confirm, amend, or set aside the decision.
(13) The relevant Authority may renew any permit upon application.
(14) The relevant Authority may, at any time, suspend or revoke a permit if it has reason to
believe that—
(a)

the holder of the permit has failed to comply with any provisions of this Act;

(b)

the holder of the permit has failed to comply with any conditions attached to the
permit;

(c) the holder of the permit had improperly or illegally obtained the permit; or
(d)

it is necessary for national interest, national security or non-proliferation purposes,
and shall notify the holder of the permit within thirty days by a notice in writing
that the permit has been suspended or revoked.

(15) A suspension or revocation under subsection (14) shall remain in force until the holder of
the permit has taken measures to the satisfaction of the relevant Authority to remove the reasons
for such suspension or revocation.
(16) Any person aggrieved due to a suspension or revocation of a permit under this section may
appeal to the relevant Authority, who may confirm, amend, or set aside the decision.
13\. Registration
(1) An application for registration to carry out brokering of controlled items referred to in
section 11 shall be made to the relevant Authority in accordance with the regulations made under
this Act.
(2) The relevant Authority may, at any time after the receipt of an application under this section
and before the application is determined, by a written notice, require the applicant to provide any
additional documents or information to the relevant Authority within the period specified in the
notice.
(3) If the additional documents or information required under subsection (2) are not provided by
the applicant within the period specified in the notice or any extended period granted by the
relevant Authority, the application shall be deemed to be withdrawn and shall not be further
proceeded with, but without affecting the right of the applicant to make a new application.
(4) The relevant Authority may, after considering the application for registration and any
additional documents or information provided under subsection (2) and being satisfied that all the
requirements have been fulfilled, register the applicant.

(5) The relevant Authority shall, after considering the application for registration and any
additional documents or information provided under subsection (2) and being satisfied that all the
requirements have not been fulfilled, refuse registration.
(6) Registration under this section shall be valid for a period of 3 years and shall be renewable
upon application.
(7) The relevant Authority may impose such conditions, as it considers appropriate in granting
the registration.
(8) The conditions imposed upon the registration may at any time be varied or amended by the
relevant Authority provided that it is necessary for national interest, national security or nonproliferation purposes and the registered broker is given a reasonable opportunity of being heard.
(9) The decision of the relevant Authority under this section shall be communicated to the
applicant in writing as soon as practicable.
(10) The written notice by the relevant Authority under subsection (9) shall specify—
(a)

in the case where the registration is granted, the fact of such registration and the
conditions, if any, imposed under subsection (7); and

(b)

in the case of a refusal of registration, the fact of such refusal and the reason for
the refusal.

(11) A person aggrieved due to a denial of a registration under this section may appeal to the
relevant Authority, who may confirm, amend or set aside the decision.
(12) The relevant Authority may, at any time, suspend or revoke a registration if it has reason to
believe that —
(a)

the registered broker has failed to comply with any provisions of this Act;

(b)

the registered broker has failed to comply with any conditions attached to the
registration;

(c)

the registered broker had improperly or illegally obtained the registration; or

(d)

it is necessary for national interest, national security or non-proliferation purposes,
and shall notify the registered broker within thirty days by a notice in writing that
the registration has been suspended or revoked.

(13) A suspension or revocation under subsection (12) shall remain in force until the registered
broker has taken measures to the satisfaction of the relevant Authority to remove the reasons for
such suspension or revocation.

(14) Any person aggrieved due to the suspension or revocation of the registration under this
section may appeal to the relevant Authority, who may confirm, amend or set aside the decision of
the suspension or revocation.
14\. Register of the relevant Authority
(1) The relevant Authority shall keep and maintain a register of permit holders and registered
brokers.
(2) The register shall be available to the public for inspection subject to such conditions as the
relevant Authority considers appropriate.
(3) A copy or extract of any entry in the register duly certified by the relevant Authority shall be
given to any person requiring the copy or extract upon payment of the prescribed fee.
(4) The register shall be conclusive evidence if any question arises as to whether any person
holds a valid permit or is a registered broker under this Act.
15\. Maintenance of Documents and Register of permit holders and registered brokers
(1) A person who is granted a permit or registered as a broker under this Act shall maintain
comprehensive records in relation to the controlled items in accordance with the regulations made
under this Act—
(a)

for a period of 5 years;

(b)

in hard copy (paper) or electronic formats.

(2) A person who is granted a permit or registered as a broker shall make these records available
to the relevant government authorities upon request.
16\. Notice of theft, loss or release of controlled items
Permit holders and registered brokers shall immediately notify the relevant Authority of the theft,
loss or release of controlled items.
17\. Emergency preparedness
The procedures laid out in the Disaster Management Act shall be followed with respect to the
mechanism that will be in place to respond to any incidents with nuclear, biological and chemical

materials, whether intentional or accidental and which could have harmful effects on human,
animal or plant health, or pose a hazard to security and the environment.
18\. Relevant Authority
(1) The relevant Authority is —
(a)

Ministry of Foreign Affairs;

(b)

## PART III

COUNTERING PROLIFERATION FINANCING
19\. Object of Part III
(1) The provisions of this Part of the Act are to—
(a)

protect the national interest and promote the security of Antigua and Barbuda by
preventing the proliferation of weapons of mass destruction; and

(b)

give effect to Article 41 of the Charter of the United Nations by implementing
financial measures arising from counter-proliferation resolutions of the United
Nations Security Council.

(2) The provisions of this Part applies—
(a)

in Antigua and Barbuda; and

(b)

to all citizens of Antigua and Barbuda and bodies corporate incorporated under the
laws of Antigua and Barbuda wherever located; and

(c)

to a vessel flying flag of Antigua and Barbuda;

(d)

to an aircraft registered in Antigua and Barbuda;

(e)

to an offence committed on board a vessel flying the flag of Antigua and Barbuda
or an aircraft registered in Antigua and Barbuda wherever located.

20\. Meaning of financial transaction
(1) A financial transaction is the transfer of an asset by any means, including physical or
electronic transfer.
(2) A person conducts a financial transaction if the person is a party to the transaction, or
procures or facilitates the transaction.
21\. Meaning of proliferation financing
(1) A person commits the offence of proliferation financing when a person—
(a)

makes available an asset; or

(b)

provides a financial service; or

(c)

conducts a financial transaction; and
the person knows that, or is reckless as to whether, the asset, financial service or
financial transaction is intended to, in whole or in part, facilitate an activity
specified in subsection (2) regardless of whether the specified activity occurs or is
attempted.

(2) The specified activities are—
(a)
the manufacture, production, possession, acquisition, stockpiling, storage,
development, transportation, sale, supply, transfer, export, transhipment or use of—
(i)

nuclear weapons; or

(ii)

chemical weapons; or

(iii)

biological weapons; or

(iv)

materials related to nuclear weapons, chemical weapons or biological

weapons that are prescribed by Regulations; or
(b)
the provision of technical training, advice, service, brokering or assistance
related to any of the activities in paragraph (a).
22 Prohibition against proliferation financing
(1) A person shall not engage in proliferation financing.
(2) A person who contravenes subsection (1) commits an offence and is liable—
(a)

on summary conviction—
(i)

(ii)

(b)

if a natural person, to a fine not exceeding three hundred thousand dollars
($300,000) or imprisonment for a term not exceeding two (2) years, or both;
or
if a body corporate, to a fine not exceeding five hundred thousand dollars
($500,000);

on conviction on indictment—
(i)

if a natural person, to a fine not exceeding five million dollars ($5,000,000)
or imprisonment for a term not exceeding twenty-five (25) years or to both;
or

(ii)

if a body corporate, to a fine not exceeding ten million dollars
($10,000,000) or twice the value of the transaction involved, whichever is
the greater.

23\. Prohibition against dealing with assets
(1) A person must not deal with an asset knowing that, or reckless as to whether, the asset is
owned, controlled or held, directly or indirectly, wholly or jointly—
(a)

by a designated person or entity; or

(b)

on behalf of a designated person or entity; or

(c)

at the direction of a designated person or entity.

(2) A person who contravenes subsection (1) is guilty of an offence, and shall be liable—
(a)

on summary conviction—

(i) if a natural person, to a fine not exceeding three hundred thousand dollars ($300,000)
or imprisonment for a term not exceeding two (2) years, or both; or
(ii)
(b)

if a body corporate, to a fine not exceeding five hundred thousand dollars
($500,000);
on conviction on indictment :
(i)

if a natural person, to a fine not exceeding eight hundred thousand dollars
($800,000) or imprisonment for a term not exceeding ten (10) years or to
both; or

(ii)

if a body corporate, to a fine not exceeding two million dollars ($2,000,000)
or twice the value of the transaction involved, whichever is the greater.

(3) For the avoidance of doubt, subsection (1) applies to any and all assets of persons and
entities listed in subsection (1) and is not limited to assets related to a specific act, plot or threat.
24\. Prohibition against making assets available
(1) A person must not make an asset available knowing that, or reckless as to whether, it is being
made available, directly or indirectly, wholly or jointly—
(a)

to a designated person or entity; or

(b)

to a person or entity owned or controlled by a designated person or entity; or

(c)

to a person or entity acting on behalf of a designated person or entity; or

(d)

for the benefit of a designated person or entity.

(2) A person who contravenes subsection (1) is guilty of an offence, and is liable—
(a)

(b)

on summary conviction:
(i)

if a natural person, to a fine not exceeding three hundred thousand dollars
($300,000) or imprisonment for a term not exceeding two (2) years, or both;
or

(ii)

if a body corporate, to a fine not exceeding five hundred thousand dollars
($500,000);

on conviction on indictment :

(c)

if a natural person, to a fine not exceeding eight hundred thousand dollars
($800,000) or imprisonment for a term not exceeding ten (10) years or to
both; or

(ii)

if a body corporate, to a fine not exceeding two million dollars ($2,000,000)
or twice the value of the transaction involved, whichever is the greater.

(3) For the purpose of subsection (1), it is immaterial whether the asset is located inside or
outside Antigua and Barbuda.

## PART IV

MISCELLANEOUS
25\. Protection of Actions Taken in Good Faith and Without Negligence
No suit, prosecution or other legal proceeding shall lie against the Government of Antigua and
Barbuda including the relevant Authority or any authorized officers for anything which is done in
good faith in pursuance of this Act or any implementing regulations.

26\. Confidentiality of Information Obtained by Government Authorities
(1) Any information and document obtained pursuant to this Act or its implementing
regulations or by virtue of this Act or its implementing regulations are privileged and confidential
within or outside Antigua and Barbuda unless such information or document is publicly available.
(2) A person in possession of privileged or confidential information or documents shall not,
without the written consent of the person from whom they were obtained, disclose, transmit or
make known to any person any privileged or confidential information or documents, allow
privileged or confidential information or documents to be disclosed, transmitted or made known to
any person, or allow any person to have access to privileged or confidential information or
documents, unless for the purpose of—
(a)

enforcement of this Act or its implementing regulations;

(b)

dealing with an emergency involving public safety;

(c)

satisfying the requirements of any law or as evidence in any court of law; or

(d)

sharing relevant information with authorities in other countries if required
for effective international cooperation.

(3) If the relevant Authority or any court is of opinion that the disclosure of certain information
or document may compromise the interests of Antigua and Barbuda or affected person, it may
direct that any proceedings before it be held in camera.

27\. Inspections
(1) The Authority is authorised to facilitate the inspection of persons subject to regulations
under this Act to ensure their compliance with this Act and any regulations issued hereunder,
including compliance with all applicable biosecurity, chemical and nuclear security measures.
(2) The Authority may designate persons or classes of persons as inspectors for the purpose of
the enforcement of this Act and may set conditions for the conduct of inspection activities.
(3) An inspector may, with the consent of the person in control of any premises or under a
warrant, enter the premises and exercise any power under subsection (1) to ensure—
(a)

that the provisions of this Act and any regulations issued hereunder have been or
are being complied with; or

(b)

that the conditions applicable to a license or permit issued under sections 11 or 12
have been or are being complied with by the license or permit holder.

(4) An inspector carrying out an inspection may—
(a)

search any premises;

(b)

operate any photographic or video-recording equipment anywhere in or around the
premises provided safety regulations in force at the premises permit doing so;

(c)

require the attendance of and question any person whom the inspector considers
will be able to assist in the inspection;

(d)

inspect or examine, take samples of, detain or remove any substance or item
considered relevant by the inspector to the enforcement of this Act;

(e)

require any person to produce for inspection, or to copy, any document that the
inspector believes contains any information relevant to the administration of this
Act;

(f)

use or cause to be used any equipment at the place to make copies of any data or
any record, book of account or other document;

(g)

use or cause to be used any computer or data processing system to examine any
data contained in or available to the computer or system;

(h)

reproduce or cause to be reproduced any record from the data, in the form of a
printout or other intelligible output, and remove the printout or other output for
examination or copying;

(i)

have operated any equipment, including electronic equipment located at the
premises;

(j)

be accompanied by an expert, as appropriate, chosen by the inspector and
authorised by the Authority; and

(k)

require that any person in control of the premises take any other reasonable
measures that the inspector considers appropriate.

Any power referred to in this subsection may only be exercised in a manner that the person in
control of the premises believes, on reasonable grounds, to be in accordance with safety
procedures applicable at the premises.
(5) An inspector, expert, or representative of the Authority—
(a)

shall be given a certificate of designation;

(b)

on entering any premises under this Act shall produce the certificate of designation
at the request of the person in control of the premises, at any reasonable time;

(c)

shall, as soon as is practicable after completing the inspection, give the person in
control of the premises a written notice stating that the premises have been
entered, if, at any time between the time of entry of any premises to be inspected
and the time the inspection is completed, there is no person appearing to be in
control of the premises, and specify the following matters—
(i)

the time and date of entry;

(ii)

the circumstances and purpose of entry; and

(iii)

the name of every person entering;

(d)

shall provide copies of any documents to the Authority;

(e)

shall, where applicable, have a warrant with him or her and produce it if required
to do so, and where any thing is seized, give the person in control of the premises a
written inventory of all things so seized;

(f)

shall provide a report of their inspection to the Authority and describe any
suspected non-compliance with this Act or the regulations issued hereunder;

(6) A person in control of a premises entered under subsections (3) and (4), and every person
present in the premises, shall give an inspector and any expert accompanying an inspector all
reasonable assistance to enable the inspector and any accompanying expert to perform his duties,
and shall furnish the inspector with any information related to the administration of this Act that
the inspector reasonably requests.
(7) The Authority may refer cases of suspected non-compliance to the Royal Police Force of
Antigua and Barbuda for investigation.

28\. Enforcement
(1) Without prejudice to the powers of the authorized officers under any other national law, the
enforcement powers provided under this section shall be available to the authorized officers of
Antigua and Barbuda. The authorized officers of Antigua and Barbuda shall have the following
powers, for the purpose of enforcement of this Act, including but not limited to—
(a)

receive and consider any report of the commission of an offence under this Act;

(b)

demand the production of any permit, record or other document under this Act;

(c)

investigate any offence under this Act;

(d)

prosecute suspected violations of this Act; and

(e)

examine and seize any controlled items or unlisted items together with any
container, package, conveyance or other article in which the controlled items or
unlisted items are stored, kept or found, record, book, account, document or
computerized data relating to any offence under this Act.

(2) The relevant Authority may collaborate with competent authorities of other States and
international organizations and entities and coordinate their actions to the extent required by the
implementation of this Act.
29\. Regulations
In addition to the regulations required elsewhere in this Act, the Minister who has authority in
relation to this Act may make such other regulations as are necessary to carry out the purposes and
provisions of this Act.
30\. Repeals
The Biological Weapons Act, CAP 52 is hereby repealed.
