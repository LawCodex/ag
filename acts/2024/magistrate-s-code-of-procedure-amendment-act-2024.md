ANTIGU A AND BARBUDA
MAGISTRATE’S CODE OF PROCEDURE (AMENDMENT) ACT 2024
No. 22 of 2024
AN ACT to amend the Magistrate’s Code of Procedure Act, Cap. 255 to provide for an increase in
the criminal penalties that a Magistrate may impose and for other connected purposes.
ENACTED by the Parliament of Antigua and Barbuda as follows―

---

### 1.

Short title
This Act may be cited as the Magistrate’s Code of Procedure (Amendment) Act 2024.

### 2.

Interpretation
In this Act –
"principal Act” means the Magistrate’s Code of Procedure Act, Cap. 255

### 3.

Amendment of section 62 – Right of accused persons to bail
Section 62 of the principal Act is amended in subsection (3) thereof by inserting into the list of
offences the following –
“Indictable offences under the Anti-Gang Act, 2024”

### 4.

Amendment of section 98 – Consecutive sentences of imprisonment
Section 98 of the principal Act is amended by repealing subsection (1) in its entirety and
substituting the following therefor –
“(1) Where a sentence of imprisonment for a summary offence is passed on any person the
Magistrate may order that the sentence shall commence at the expiration of any other term of
imprisonment to which that person has been previously sentenced, so, however, that where two
or more sentences of a Magistrate are ordered to run consecutively the aggregate term of
imprisonment shall not exceed five years, unless such sentences include at least –
(a)

two sentences for indictable offences dealt with summarily by consent; or

(b)

a sentence for an offence for which the Magistrate was empowered to order a term
of imprisonment of more than six months, in which case the aggregate terms of
imprisonment shall not exceed seven years.”

### 5.

Insertion of section 100 - Power to remit matter tried in Magistrate’s Court to High
Court for sentencing
The principal Act is amended by inserting after section 99 the following –
“100. Committal for sentence on summary trial of offence triable either way
(1) When a person aged 18 years or over is convicted on summary trial of an offence that is
triable either way, the Magistrate may send the offender to the High Court to be sentenced if the
Magistrate is satisfied that –
(a)

the offence; or

(b)

A combination of the offence and one or more offences associated with it; or

(c)

the evidence disclosed in the case, together with the report on the offender’s
character and antecedents; are so serious that the sentencing power of the
Magistrate are not sufficient to deal with the offender.

(2) The Magistrate may commit the offender either in custody or on bail to the High Court for
sentencing.
(3) Where a Magistrate commits an offender to the High Court under subsection (1), the
Magistrate shall send to the Registrar of the High Court (a)

a copy signed by the Magistrate of the Minute or Memorandum of the conviction
entered in the register;

(b)

a copy of any note of evidence given at the trial of the offender, any written
statement tendered in evidence and any deposition;

(c)

such documents and articles produced in evidence before the Magistrate as have
been retained by the Magistrate; and

(d)

any report relating to the offender that was considered by the Magistrate.

(4) The High Court –
(a)

must inquire into the circumstances of the case; and

(b)

may deal with the offender in the same way as if the offender had been convicted
before the High Court on indictment.”

### 6.

Amendment of section 147 – Venue
Section 147 of the principal Act is repealed in its entirety and replace as follows –
“147. Venue
All civil proceedings shall be heard at the Magistrate’s Court located in Saint John’s.”

### 7.

Commencement
This Act shall come into effect on the 16th day of September, 2024.
