PREVENTION OF TERRORISM (AMENDMENT) ACT 2024
No. 12 of 2024
AN ACT to amend the Prevention of Terrorism Act 2005, No. 12 of 2005 to strengthen the legal
framework relating to the prevention of terrorism and countering terrorist financing and for other
connected purposes.
ENACTED by the Parliament of Antigua and Barbuda as follows―
Short title
This Act may be cited as the Prevention of Terrorism (Amendment) Act 2024.
Interpretation
In this Act, “principal Act” means the Prevention of Terrorism Act 2005, No. 12 of 2005

AMENDMENTS TO PART II – Specified Entity
Amendment of section 3 – Identifying and Designating Specified Entities
Section 3 of the principal Act is repealed and replaced as follows –
“3. Identifying and designating specified entities
(1) A person or entity may be designated a Specified Entity –

(a)

if the person or entity is the subject of a Specified Entity Declaration issued by the
Attorney General pursuant to section 3A; or

(b)

if the person or entity has been declared by resolution of the United Nations Security
Council or any of its relevant Committees as a Specified Entity;

(c)

at the request of the competent authority of a foreign country where the request is
supported by reasonable grounds or a reasonable basis to suspect or to believe that
the designee meets the prescribed criteria for designation.

(2) A person or entity shall not be designated a specified entity unless the person or entity satisfies
the designation criteria prescribed in the regulations.
(3) A Specified Entity Declaration shall be published in the Gazette immediately upon the
designation being made in accordance with this Act.”
Amendment of the principal Act – Insertion of sections 3A to 3K
The principal Act is amended by inserting after section 3 the following new sections “3A. Issue of a Specified Entity Declaration by the Attorney General
(1) The Attorney General may issue a Specified Entity Declaration against a subject where –
(a)

an Investigating Authority has reasonable grounds to suspect or a reasonable basis
to believe that a proposed subject satisfies the prescribed designation criteria for
declaring a subject to be a specified entity;

(b)

the Investigating Authority recommends to the Attorney General that a Specified
Entity Declaration be made in respect of the subject; and

(c)

the Attorney General is satisfied that there are reasonable grounds or there is a
reasonable basis for making a Specified Entity Declaration.

(1) The issuing of a Specified Entity Declaration shall not be dependent on the existence of criminal
proceedings having been commenced or about to commence against the subject.
(2) Where a Specified Entity Declaration has been made against a subject, the prohibitions outlined
in sections 7A and 7B shall apply to that subject.

3B. Designation of a specified entity by United Nations Security Council Resolutions
(1) Where the United Nations Security Council or any of its relevant committees by resolution
declares a subject to be a specified entity, then the subject shall be deemed to be a specified entity
in Antigua and Barbuda upon publication in the Gazette of an Order by the Minister of Foreign
Affairs that the subject is a specified entity.
(2) Where an Order under subsection (1) has been made against a subject, the prohibitions outlined
in sections 7A and 7B shall apply to that subject.
3C. Designation of specified entity at the request of a foreign country
(1) The Minister of Foreign Affairs is the appropriate Minister for the purpose of receiving request
from the competent authority of a foreign country for designation of a specified entity; and for
communicating a request from Antigua and Barbuda to the competent authority of a foreign country
for designation of a subject.
(2) Where a request is received from a competent authority of a foreign country to designate a
subject as a Specified Entity, the Minister of Foreign Affairs shall forthwith communicate the
requests to the Attorney General.
(3) The Attorney General shall review the requests to ensure that –
(a)

it is supported by adequate information to allow the Investigating Authority in
Antigua and Barbuda to identify the subject with certainty;

(b)

it is supported by affidavit evidence showing that there are reasonable grounds or a
reasonable basis for believing that the subject should be designated a specified
entity;

(c)

the subject satisfies the prescribed designation criteria for declaring a subject to be
a specified entity; and

(d)

the request is accompanied by details of the procedure
that may be followed by the subject to have the
designation revoked.

(4) Where the Attorney General is satisfied that there are reasonable grounds or a reasonable
basis for believing that the subject should be designated a specified entity, the Attorney General
shall declare the subject to be a specified entity.

(5) The decision to designate a subject as a specified entity as a result of a request from a competent
authority of a foreign country shall not be dependent on the existence of criminal proceedings having
been commenced or about to commence against the subject in the foreign country.
(6) Where a Specified Entity Declaration has been made against a subject, the prohibitions outlined
in sections 7A and 7B shall apply to that subject.
3D. Freezing Funds and Other Assets of Specified Entities
(1)

The Attorney General shall, upon the designation of a subject as a specified entity, issue a
Freezing Order requiring all natural and legal persons to forthwith freeze the funds and other
assets of a subject who has been designated a specified entity under section 3A, 3B or 3C.

(2)

The freeze order granted under subsection (1) may explicitly include any sanctions or
prohibitions listed in sections 7A and 7B.

(3)

The freezing order shall apply to –
(a)

all funds or other assets that are owned or controlled by the designated person or
entity;

(b)

those funds or other assets that are wholly or jointly owned or controlled, directly
or indirectly, by the designated person or entity; and

(c)

the funds or other assets derived or generated from funds or other assets owned or
controlled directly or indirectly by the designated person or entity; as well as,

(d)

funds or other assets of persons and entities acting on behalf of, or at the direction
of, a designated person or entity.

(4) Where the designation of the specified entity was done pursuant to section 3B or 3C, the freezing
order issued by the Attorney General shall remain in effect until such time as the subject is de-listed
in accordance with the procedures prescribed in regulations issued pursuant to this Act, or the
Specified Entity Declaration is revoked by the relevant designating authority.

(5) Where the designation was done pursuant to section 3A, the freezing order issued by the
Attorney General shall, subject to subsection (6), be a temporary order having effect for a period of
60 days from the date of the Order unless sooner discharged by the High Court.

(6) The Attorney General may apply to the High Court of Justice, before the expiration of the
temporary order to have the freezing order confirmed by the court.

(7) The application under subsection (6) may be made ex part
3E. Administrative Review of Specified Entity Declarations of the Attorney General
(1) A Specified Entity Declaration under section 3A may at any time be varied or revoked by the
Attorney General.
(2) If the Attorney General determines that a subject does not fit the prescribed criteria for being
designated a specified entity, the Attorney General shall revoke the declaration.
(3) A subject who has been designated a specified entity under section 3A, or the personal
representative of the estate of a subject who was designated a specified entity may, in person
or by a representative, request the Attorney General to revoke or vary the Specified Entity
Declaration on the basis of –
(a)

a mistake of identity; or

(b)

the person or entity no longer meets the criteria prescribed for declaration,

(c)

the individual is confirmed to be deceased; or

(d)

the entity has ceased to exist.

(4) A person who is affected by the designation of a subject as a specified entity or whose property
is affected by a freezing order, as a consequence of a designation, may apply to the Attorney General

(a)

there has been a mismatch of identity with that of the specified entity, or

(b)

the property frozen is not owned by, under the control of, or is being held on behalf
of the specified entity.

(5) The Attorney General, upon receiving a request under this section, shall decide whether to vary
or revoke the designation or to take no action with respect to it.
(6) The Attorney General shall notify the applicant of his decision within 30 days of receiving the
request.
(7) If the Attorney General does not give a decision within the time specified, or, if the applicant is
dissatisfied with the decision of the Attorney General, the applicant may appeal the designation or
the decision of the Attorney General to a Judge in the High Court.

3F. Appeals to the Court
(1) An appeal under this section may be made on the grounds that:
(a)

the declaration of the specified entity does not meet the criteria under the
regulations;

(b)

there has been a mismatch of the identity of the person affected with the person
intended to be declared a specified entity;

(c)

property frozen as a result of the specified entity designation is not property of the
specified entity because it is not owned by, under the control of, nor held on behalf
of the specified entity, neither is it terrorist property.

(2) The appeal may be dealt with in Chambers.

3H. Authorization for Specified Entity to Access Frozen Funds, other Assets or Economic

AMENDMENTS TO PART VII – Miscellaneous
12\. Amendment of section 34A – Tipping off
Section 34A of the principal Act is amended by repealing the section in its entirety and replacing it
as follows –
“34A. Tipping off
(1) It is an offence for a person to disclose that an investigation into allegations of terrorism, the
financing of terrorism or terrorist property is being contemplated or carried out if that disclosure is
likely to prejudice that investigation.
(2) It is an offence for a person to disclose to a third party that he has made, is about to make or is
contemplating making a suspicious activity report pursuant to section 34(4) or otherwise to the

ONDCP, the Police or to any other law enforcement agency, if that disclosure might prejudice any
investigation that might be carried out as a result of the making of the suspicious activity report.
(3) Where a financial institution forms a suspicion that a customer or transaction is related to terrorist
financing and reasonably believes that performing the customer due diligence process will tip off
the customer, the financial institution may forego the customer due diligence process and instead
file a suspicious activity report under section 34(4).
(4) A person who commits an offence under this section is liable on summary conviction to a fine
not exceeding eight hundred thousand dollars ($800,000) or to imprisonment for a term not
exceeding three years, or to both.”

15\. Amendment of the principal Act – Insertion of section 45 National Sanctions Secretariat
The principal Act is amended by inserting after section 44 the following –
“45. National Sanctions Secretariat
“45. National Sanctions Secretariat
(1)

There is hereby established a National Sanctions Secretariat, (herein “the Secretariat”)
under the oversight of the Minister of Foreign Affairs.

(2)

The Secretariat shall be responsible for the implementation of all United Nations measures
under Article 41 of the UN Charter that calls for the implementation of targeted financial

(3)

For purposes of this Act, the Secretariat shall be responsible for implementation of targeted
financial sanctions relating to terrorism and the prohibitions that take effect upon a subject

(4)

The Secretariat shall have the following members:
(a)

The Director of the ONDCP, the Chairman,

(b)

an attorney-at-law of the Ministry of Legal Affairs,

(c)

an officer of the Ministry of Foreign Affairs,

(d)

an officer of an appropriate unit of the ONDCP,

(e)

the editor of the electronic version of the official Gazette.

(5) The Chairman, or in his absence the person appointed as Deputy Chairman, shall coordinate
the activities of the Secretariat and report to the Minister of Foreign Affairs.
(6) The Minister may on a permanent or ad hoc basis add members to the Secretariat.
(7) The Secretariat’s function shall be in support of the competent authorities and their assigned
duties under this Act and to effectively implement the sanctions regime and the financial and
economic prohibitions relating to specified entities under this Act and its regulations.
(8) The Secretariat shall perform its functions and exercise powers, including authority delegated
to it by the Minister under subsection (9), to support the appropriate Minister in having
oversight of the implementation of the sanctions regime under this Act. This includes but is
not limited to:
(a)

maintaining a “sanctions website” with publicly available information relating to
specified entities under this Act;

(b)

maintaining, keeping updated and providing public access to a Consolidated List of
all specified entities declared in accordance with section 3A or deemed as such by
operation of section 3B of this Act;

(c)

providing guidance to persons affected by the implementation of the sanctions
regime, including developing and publishing the Guidance listed in subsection (10);

(d)

publishing such forms and notices as are necessary for the implementation of the
sanctions regime under this Act;

(e)

developing protocols necessary or conducive to the efficient implementation of the
sanctions regime;

(f)

facilitating the sharing of information about the sanctions regime with other
agencies or bodies as provided by law.

(9) The Minister of Foreign Affairs may delegate in writing to the Secretariat the exercise of any
or all of his powers and functions under this Act, other than the power of delegation conferred by
this subsection, the power to declare a specified entity under section 3, and the revocation and
variation power under section 3A.”
(10) The Secretariat shall issue guidelines publishable online for financial institutions and the
general public on the subject of specified entities and prohibitions that relate to them.
(11) The Secretariat shall:
(a)

issue advisories to financial institutions based on:
(i)

FATF statements relating to High Risk jurisdictions subject to a call for
action;

(ii)

FATF statements on jurisdictions under increased monitoring due to strategic
deficiencies in their AML/CFT regimes;

(iii)

CFATF statements on jurisdictions under the CFATF International
Cooperation Review Group Process.

(b)

require countermeasures to be taken by financial institutions with advice of the
ONDCP.

(c)

notify the Financial Secretary and Regulators of financial institutions of all
advisories and countermeasures issued under this subsection.

(12) Members of the Secretariat shall be remunerated in accordance with a Cabinet decision.
16\. Mechanisms for Cooperation and Coordination
The principal Act is amended by inserting after section 45 the following new section –
“46. Mechanisms for Cooperation and Coordination
The competent authorities for implementation of the sanctions regime should develop, agree and
document such memoranda of understanding, protocols and cooperative measures to more
effectively implement the sanctions regime.
