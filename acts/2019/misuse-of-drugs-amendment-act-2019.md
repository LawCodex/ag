AN ACT to amend the Misuse of Drugs Act, Cap. 283.
ENACTED by the Parliament of Antigua and Barbuda as follows—
1\. Short Title
This Act may be cited as the Misuse of Drugs (Amendment) Act, 2019
2\. Interpretation
In this Act—
“principal Act” means the Misuse of Drugs Act, Cap. 283.
3\. Amendment to Section 2 of the Act
Section 2 of the principal Act is amended by inserting in alphabetical order the following
definitions—
“Council” means the National Drug Council established under section 3A of this Act;
4\. Insertion of new Part IA – National Drug Council
The principal Act is amended by inserting after Part I, a new Part as follows—
“PART IA NATIONAL DRUG COUNCIL

3A. National Drug Council
(1) There is hereby established a National Drug Council, hereinafter referred to as the Council.
(2) The Council shall consist of the following persons—
(i)

the Commissioner of Police of the Royal Antigua and Barbuda Police
Force or his nominee;

(ii)

the Director of the Office of the National Drugs and Money Laundering
Control Policy or his nominee;

(iii)

the Chief of Defence Staff of the Antigua and Barbuda Defence Force or his
nominee;

(iv)

the Director of Analytical Services or his nominee;

(v)

a person nominated by the Minister with responsibility for Health;

(vi)

a person nominated by the Minister with responsibility for Social
Transformation;

(vii)

a person nominated by the Minister with responsibility for Education;

(viii)

a person nominated by the Minister with responsibility for National
Security;

(ix)

a person from the Barbuda Council nominated by the Minister with
responsibility for Barbuda Affairs;

(x)

a person with training in drug addiction, counselling and rehabilitation
employed at a rehabilitation centre in Antigua and Barbuda nominated by
the Minister with responsibility for Social Transformation; and

(xi)

a person representing the Antigua Christian Council or other religious
organization appointed by the Minister with responsibility for
Ecclesiastical Affairs who shall not serve for more than one year and may
be eligible for reappointment three years after their first appointment.

3B. Powers and duties of the Council
(1) The Council shall conduct a regular review with respect to drugs that are misused and where
such misuse may have harmful effects on the users, either physically or mentally.

(2) The Council shall advise the Minister on measures which should be taken to prevent the
misuse of such drugs or dealing with social problems connected with their misuse, and in
particular on measures which shall be taken—
(a)

to restrict the availability of such drugs or to supervise the arrangement of their
supply;

(b)

for enabling persons affected by the misuse of drugs to obtain proper advice;

(c)

for securing the provision of proper facilities and services for the treatment,
rehabilitation and after-care of such persons;

(d)

for promoting co-operation between various professional and community services
involved in dealing with social problems connected with the misuse of such drugs;

(e)

for educating young persons and other members of the public about the danger of
misusing such drugs, and for raising public awareness about the dangers of those
drugs; and

(f)

for promoting research or obtaining information about a matter which in the opinion
of the Council has relevance for the purpose of preventing the misuse of such drugs
or dealing with any social problems connected with their misuse.

(3) The Council shall consider any matter relating to drug dependence, the misuse of drugs or
the illegal supply of drugs which may be referred to it by the Minster.
(4) The Cabinet shall appoint a Chairman and Deputy Chairman from among the members of
the Council.
(5) Six members of the Council shall constitute a quorum provided the Chairman or Deputy
Chairman is present, and subject to that quorum, the Council may determine its own procedure.
(6) The Council is responsible for the—
(a)

National Drug Control Policy which is a document that includes—
(i)

the legislative framework and a mandate;

(ii)

an organizational structure;

(iii)

a budget;

(iv)

measures to deny safe havens to criminals;

(v)

measures to control the movement of illegal drugs and precursor chemicals;

(b)

a National Drug Control Strategy which is a document that includes its—
(i)

formulation and design;

(ii)

contents and budgets; and

(iii)

implication and monitoring.

(7) The Council may appoint one or more Technical Working Groups or any number of subcommittees to report on any matter referred to them by the Council.
(8) The Council shall be supported in its work by the Executive Secretary referred to in section
3C.
3C. Appointment and duties of the Executive Secretary
(1) The Council shall appoint an Executive Secretary.
(2) There shall be paid to the Executive Secretary, such remuneration as the Cabinet may
determine.
(3) The Executive Secretary shall perform the following duties—
(a)

prepare meeting schedules and the proposed agenda in coordination with the
Chairman or the Deputy Chairman;

(b)

prepare and disseminate meeting invitations, meeting agendas and minutes of the
Council’s previous immediate meeting at least one week prior to the date of an
upcoming meeting;

(c)

prepare portfolios of all documents the Council may need in its meetings and
provide copies to all members at least one week prior to the date of the meeting;

(d)

liaise with the Drug Observatory and the Drug Information Network of Antigua and
Barbuda (DINAB) at the Office of National Drug and Money Laundering Control
Policy (ONDCP) to collect, collate scientific facts and data on subjects of research
and discussion by the Council;

(e)

prepare the minutes of meetings;

(f)

codify the recommendations and decisions of the Council for ratification by persons
who attended the meeting;

(g)

disseminate decisions issued by the Council;

(h)

monitor the implementation of the recommendations of the Council and evaluate the
outcomes;

(i)

prepare bi-annual reports of the Council’s achievements;

(j)

attend national, regional and international meetings relating to the issue of drugs in
the hemisphere at the request of the Chairman or in his absence, the Deputy
Chairman;

(k)

participate in, and facilitate the implementation of recommendations of, regional
and international bodies, at the request of the Chairman or in his absence, the Deputy
Chairman, such as, but is not limited to, the Mutual Evaluation Mechanism (MEM)
and CARICOM IMPACS; and

(l)

support the preparation, implementation and evaluation of the National Anti-Drug
Strategy Plan.

3D. Appointment and duties of a Technical Working Group
(1) The Council may appoint one or more Technical Working Groups or any number of subcommittees to report on any matter referred to them by the Council.
(2) The duties of a Technical Working Group are as follows—
(a)

Demand Reduction which is a task where a Technical Working Group is
responsible for—
(i)

integrated demand reduction;

(ii)

health and family life education;

(iii)

substance abuse in the workplace;

(iv)

public awareness;

(v)

networking; training and data;

(vi)

treatment and rehabilitation;

(vii) in-prison programmers;
(viii) national surveys;
(ix)

evaluation;

(b)

(c)

Supply Reduction which is a task where a Technical Working Group is responsible
for—
(i)

sharing of information and intelligence;

(ii)

law enforcement training enhancement;

(iii)

intelligence gathering;

(iv)

monitoring and control of precursor and essential chemicals;

(v)

forensic science facilities;

International Cooperation which is a task where a Technical Working Group is
responsible for—
(i)

promoting the ratification or accession to regional and international treaties
and conventions relevant to the fight against drug trafficking and related
offences;

(ii)

promoting bi-lateral and multi-lateral agreements promoting information
sharing and assistance to other states conducting drug trafficking
investigations;

(iii)

promoting international best practices related to special treatment for minor
drug offences such as by a Drug Treatment Court;

(iv)

advocating for the establishment monitoring and maintenance of treatment
programmers and facilities to serve persons with problematic drug issues;

(v)

supporting the secure exchange of information relative to trafficking
activities in the jurisdiction with regional and international counterparts;

(d) Control Measures, which is a task where a Technical Working Group is responsible
for—
(i)

establishing and executing the appropriate strategies to empower law
enforcement agencies to apply counter-measures that will reduce the
occurrence of drug trafficking and related illicit activities;

(ii)

instituting measures to avert the diversion of controlled chemical substance,
precursors, pre-precursors and post-precursors from illicit drug activities;

(iii) adopting or strengthening comprehensive and balanced programmes aimed
at preventing and reducing drug trafficking, in accordance with the
territorial realities of Antigua and Barbuda and respecting human rights;
(iv)

adopting or strengthening control measures to prevent diversion of
controlled chemical substances illicit activities;

(v)

adopting or strengthening control measures to prevent the diversion of
pharmaceutical products containing precursor substances or those
containing narcotic drugs or psychotropic substances towards illicit
activities;

(vi)

strengthening national measures to address the challenge of new
psychotropic substances and the threat of amphetamine stimulants;

(vii) strengthening national information gathering systems and mechanisms for
exchanging intelligence information to detect routes and methods used by
criminal drug trafficking organizations;
(e)

Institutional Strengthening, which is a task where a Technical Working Group is
responsible for—
(i)

improving implementation and coordination of the National Anti-Drug
Strategy by utilizing available media outlets;

(ii)

strengthening technical and human resource capacities of stakeholders to
deliver on their mandates;

(iii)

promoting the improvement of performance of agencies undertaking
activities within the National Anti-Drug Strategy;

(iv)

promoting an evidence-based approach towards the development of policy
and programme formulation within national agencies;

(v)

promoting national compliance with international obligations;

(vi)

instituting operational plans to facilitate the achievement of the goals of the
National Anti-Drug Strategy;

(vi)

instituting a performance management framework for the National AntiDrug Strategy; and

(vii) developing the technical capacity to design and conduct drug control
programmes.
